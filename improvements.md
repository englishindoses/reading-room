# Student reader improvements

Prioritized by impact on reading *Sam and the Red Bag* independently. The review covered all 14 story pages on a 390 × 844 phone viewport and the illustrated layout at 1440 × 900 on a laptop.

1. **Make each phone page readable and turnable in one view.** At 390 × 844, Next starts below the visible area on all 14 story pages. Keep Previous and Next in a compact bottom bar that reserves its own space. Reduce the reader chrome and size the illustration to leave room for the sentence. When a short screen or enlarged text requires scrolling, keep the controls reachable without covering the content.
2. **Give the laptop reader the proportions of a book page.** The paper is 800 px wide in the laptop view, while the illustration on page 4 is about 346 px wide. Use the artwork and readable text width to size a narrower, centered portrait page with modest margins. Align the title, progress, and page-turn controls with that page so the whole reader feels like one object. Let activity forms use more width where they need it.
3. **Give the story a clear ending and a choice about practice.** After the final sentence, Next currently opens “Middle sounds.” Show “Story complete” with a clear choice to start the activities, reread, or return to the book. Track story completion and activity progress independently so a learner can stop after reading and still see what they accomplished.
4. **Make the saved page easy to get back to.** Have the library’s Continue button open the saved place directly, with a cue such as “Continue story · page 8.” Keep reading and activity resume positions separate so opening an exercise does not replace the learner’s place in the story.
5. **Put progress and page jumping in the same control.** Make “Page 4 of 14” open a clearly labeled page picker with the current page visible. Use a wrapping grid or list on phones so later pages are easy to find. Give activities their own labeled section and keep Library, Collections, and Profile out of the picker.
6. **Add on-demand sentence audio.** Let a learner try reading first, then tap a clearly labeled control to hear or replay the line. Use recorded narration that works offline for the bundled story. Add individual word playback where pronunciation support is useful.
7. **Offer reading settings inside the reader.** Add an “Aa” control for text size and spacing, remember choices per profile, and make larger text reflow cleanly. Put the existing page animation preference within reach here as well so students can choose a simple turn without leaving the book.
8. **Support quick page turns from the keyboard.** Let Left and Right move through story pages, with visible controls for touch and mouse users. Keep those shortcuts out of activity fields and open menus. Announce the new page and bring keyboard and screen-reader users to its content after a turn.
9. **Make the sentence easy to find on every page.** Give the text a consistent position close to the artwork, with enough room to enlarge it. Keep the page number in the reader toolbar and reclaim the space used by the large “Page 4” heading and decorative rule.
10. **Let learners inspect the illustrations on demand.** If the picture is compact in the reading view, offer a tap-to-enlarge view. Closing it should return the learner to the same page and position.
11. **Reduce the prominence of routine save feedback.** Keep “Saved on this device” available in the reader toolbar without giving it a dedicated floating position. Make an unsuccessful save prominent and explain how to retry.
12. **Make the exit label specific.** Replace “Back” with “Book details” so the learner knows where the button leads. Keep it in the same position throughout the story and activities.
